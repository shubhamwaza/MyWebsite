03 Two-Sided Marketplace Strategy

Balancing two distinct mental models without creating two fragmented apps

A gig marketplace cannot succeed by optimizing solely for the worker or the business. We designed around the symbiotic exchange: businesses need speed and dependability, while workers need flexibility and verified trust.

Supply · The Flexible Worker

College Student & Gig Worker

"Tell me exactly what I'll get paid and where I need to travel. I can't afford to waste an evening on a fake job."
  • Needs: Flexible hours around classes, legitimate employer verification, fast shift payouts.
  • Frictions: Opaque pay, shady WhatsApp postings, travel ambiguity, tedious 8-step registration forms.
  • Mental Model: "Is this legitimate, worth my time, and close enough to reach?"
Demand · The Hiring Manager

Retail, Event & Café Operator

"I don't need a corporate recruitment portal. I have an exhibition tomorrow and need 4 reliable hands who will actually show up."
  • Needs: Fast shift fulfillment, candidate punctuality signals, minimal admin friction.
  • Frictions: No-shows leaving venues stranded, phone tag over WhatsApp, unverified identities.
  • Mental Model: "Who is verified and confirmed to show up on time?"
HMW 01 · Trust

How might we establish mutual trust before either party leaves their home?

Visible verification badges, clear employer identity signals, and transparent shift commitments built directly into the UI.

HMW 02 · Speed

How might businesses fill urgent shifts in minutes without sacrificing quality?

Pre-configured role templates, single-tap applicant reviews, and availability matching that bypasses back-and-forth messaging.

HMW 03 · Discovery

How might workers filter opportunities against real personal constraints?

One-tap mobile filter chips prioritizing distance radius, exact shift hours, and guaranteed payout rather than buried form dropdowns.

HMW 04 · Transparency

How might we prevent disputes before a shift even begins?

Strict information architecture displaying required dress code, exact duties, break policies, and payout timelines on every card.

05 Critical UX Decisions

Deliberate trade-offs that defended user momentum

Designing for busy workers and stressed hiring managers meant ruthlessly stripping unnecessary friction while strengthening trust touchpoints.

Scrapped Model

8-Step Comprehensive Onboarding Wall

Requiring ID scans, bank details, emergency contacts, and experience histories before allowing workers to even browse available shifts.

Adopted Architecture

Progressive Disclosure with Value-First Browsing

Workers browse immediately to see real earning opportunities. Verification is requested only at the moment of application commitment, when intent is proven.

Scrapped Model

Deep Full-Screen Modal Filter Sheets

Opening multi-tiered filter drawers with nested sliders for radius, wage ranges, and date pickers that broke scanning flow.

Adopted Architecture

High-Velocity Horizontal Filter Chips

One-tap toggle chips ("Today", "Near Me < 5km", "High Pay ₹4000+") pinned to the header, letting users refine results with single thumb interactions.

Scrapped Model

Generic Job Board Cards

Displaying lengthy company blurbs, tags, and ambiguous "Competitive" pay statements that forced users to click through every listing.

Adopted Architecture

Decision-First Shift Cards

Total guaranteed payout (e.g. ₹5,000), exact shift hours (10:00–21:00), transit distance (22 min away), and verified employer badge visible at zero clicks.

06 Designing for Reality

Handling the unideal paths with empathy and clarity

A real-world marketplace experiences network drops, zero-result queries, document rejections, and last-minute cancellations. We designed every failure mode as a clear recovery path.

Empty States

Zero Shifts in Selected Radius

Never a dead end. Explains the exact constraint, allows 1-tap expansion from 5km to 15km, and offers push notifications for nearby openings.

Verification States

Document Rejection & Recovery

Never a vague "Verification Failed". Highlights the exact document failure reason (e.g. glare/blur) with direct re-upload affordances.

Offline Resilience

Offline Confirmed Shift Pass

Confirmed shift location, supervisor contact, and venue directions are cached locally so workers aren't stranded if reception drops on transit.

Cancellation UX

Employer Emergency Cancellation

Immediate transparent notice with guaranteed compensation policy and an automated feed of alternative nearby shifts for that time slot.

Reliability Tracking

Verified On-Site Live Check-In

Protects attendance and reliability scores through verified on-site supervisor check-in and GPS geo-validation.

Timers & Urgency

3-Hour Transit & Shift Reminder

Tiered operational countdown with live transit travel times and supervisor details to ensure zero accidental no-shows.

07 Retrospective & Next Steps

Honest reflections and the path toward production

Great product design demands acknowledging testing limitations and building a phased roadmap for validation.

1. Quantifying Trust in User Testing

While qualitative research proved the need for verification, I would introduce structured Likert-scale confidence testing across test cohorts to measure exact perceived safety with and without verification badges.

2. Cold-Start Marketplace Simulation

Every two-sided marketplace faces the chicken-and-egg problem. In future iterations, I would prototype targeted localized launch models (e.g., launching exclusively for festival crew or college campus zones).

3. Phase 2: Autonomous Matching Intelligence

Evolving the manual search flow into automated matching that learns worker transit preferences and availability patterns to push tailored shift reservations proactively.

4. Phase 3: Instant Escrow Settlement

Integrating verified digital escrow where employer payment is locked upon shift posting and automatically disbursed to the worker's UPI wallet upon geo-verified check-out.
